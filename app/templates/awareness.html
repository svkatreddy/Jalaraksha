Water Conservation in Everyday Life

Conserving water does not require expensive technology or drastic disruption. Consistent, small daily actions across homes, campuses, and neighborhoods prevent gallons of treated water from being lost.

1. At Home: Responsible Domestic Use

Turn off taps while brushing or soaping

A running tap discharges between 6 to 9 litres of clean water per minute. Shutting the valve while brushing teeth, scrubbing dishes, or washing hands saves several buckets of water daily per person.

Inspect and repair household leaks promptly

A single dripping tap leaking one drop per second can lose over 20 litres of clean water every day. Worn rubber washers, loose tap spouts, and leaking toilet cistern valves can be repaired quickly with standard plumbing parts.

Clean efficiently with buckets instead of open hoses

Using a bucket and cloth to clean vehicles and floors uses roughly 80% less water than an open, pressurized garden hose left running continuously.

Reuse suitable greywater safely

Rinse water from vegetables and pulse washing can safely be collected and poured onto ornamental plants and garden beds. Note: Never use greywater for drinking or direct food preparation.

2. At School and College: Institutional Stewardship

Report campus leaks and tank overflows immediately

Overhead institutional tanks often overflow unobserved for hours during morning refill pumping cycles. Immediately alerting facility maintenance prevents thousands of litres from being squandered.

Careful use of shared facilities and water coolers

Always ensure cooler push-taps and washroom taps have fully sealed after drinking or washing. Never prop taps open with objects.

Display visual awareness reminders

Student clubs and volunteers can place respectful, concise reminder notices above washroom basins and drinking water stations to build a campus culture of conservation.

Engage in active student service projects

Participate in campus plumbing audits and surveys like JalaRaksha to identify structural issues and document real findings for administration review.

3. In the Community: Shared Public Outlets

Document damaged public taps and standposts

Broken street standposts in public markets and roadsides frequently suffer damage. Documenting the location through JalaRaksha or contacting the local municipality expedites repair work.

Promote appropriate rainwater harvesting (RWH)

Catchment systems, rooftop gutters, and recharge pits help replenish groundwater tables. Ensure collection pits are covered and mesh filters are maintained to prevent mosquito breeding and contamination.

Keep storm drains clear of plastic and debris

Blocked stormwater channels lead to surface runoff flooding rather than healthy groundwater recharge. Disposing of plastic waste properly protects local waterways.

Safe practices: Never claim non-potable water is safe to drink

Recycled, harvested, or greywater must only be used for gardening, construction, or flushing unless formally certified through municipal lab testing.
