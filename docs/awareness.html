Water Conservation in Everyday Life

Conserving water does not require expensive technology or drastic disruption. Consistent, small daily actions across homes, campuses, and neighborhoods prevent thousands of litres from being lost.

1. At Home: Responsible Domestic Use

Turn off taps while brushing or soaping

A running tap discharges between 6 to 9 litres of clean water per minute. Shutting the valve while brushing teeth or scrubbing dishes saves multiple buckets daily.

Inspect and repair household leaks promptly

A single dripping tap leaking one drop per second loses over 20 litres of clean water every day. Worn washers and leaking cistern valves can be repaired quickly with standard plumbing parts.

Clean with buckets instead of open hoses

Using a bucket and cloth to clean vehicles uses roughly 80% less water than an open, running hosepipe.

Reuse suitable greywater safely

Rinse water from vegetables and pulse washing can safely be collected and poured onto garden beds. Note: Never use greywater for drinking.

2. At School and College: Institutional Stewardship

Report campus leaks and tank overflows immediately

Overhead institutional tanks often overflow for hours during morning refill cycles. Immediately alerting facility staff prevents thousands of litres from being wasted.

Careful use of shared facilities and water coolers

Always ensure cooler push-taps and washroom taps have sealed completely after drinking. Never prop taps open with objects.

Display visual awareness reminders

Student volunteers can place concise reminder notices above washroom basins to build a campus culture of conservation.

3. In the Community: Shared Public Outlets

Document damaged public taps and standposts

Broken street standposts in public markets frequently suffer damage. Documenting the location through JalaRaksha expedites community awareness and repair.

Promote appropriate rainwater harvesting (RWH)

Catchment systems, rooftop gutters, and recharge pits help replenish groundwater tables. Ensure collection pits are covered to prevent contamination.
